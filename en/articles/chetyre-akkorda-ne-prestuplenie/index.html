Four chords aren’t a crime

The chords are acquitted.

Only a boring song faces criminal charges.

There’s a particular kind of conversation: someone hears a good pop track, recognizes the familiar harmony, and announces that it’s “only four chords.” As if they’ve cracked the case.

Quantity isn’t complexity

A song lives on several layers. Harmony is one of them. There’s melody, rhythm, texture, dynamics, lyrics, pauses, arrangement, tone. Four chords can support some very complex work on top.

Where the work happens

Melody

A single chord feels new when the vocal changes its target notes and rhythm.

Bass

A different note underneath changes the direction of the loop.

Dynamics

A quiet verse and the same loop in a huge chorus are two different events.

Texture

Open strings, muting, octaves, noise, stops.

When it really is boring

When every instrument repeats the loop from the first second to the last. Bass on the roots, guitar on the same rhythm, vocals sticking to safe notes. The problem isn’t the “four.” Nobody brought a second idea.

Repetition gets boring when nothing around it changes.

Five cheap tricks

01
Take the guitar out of the first verse

Its return will become an event.

02
A pause before the chorus

Half a second of silence can hit harder than a new chord.

03
Change register

The same loop played higher on the guitar can open up the chorus.

04
Let the bass argue

It doesn’t have to live on the root notes.

05
Break one bar

A shortened transition wakes the progression up.

verdict

If four chords hold the song together, leave them alone and work on the chorus.
