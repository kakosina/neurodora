The language of cute rock

How to write a line that sounds like someone said it, not like someone “composed” it.

the main ruleTalk with your mouth.

A line can be beautiful. It just shouldn’t ask for applause for its beauty.

The language of cute rock is easily ruined by the desire to write “good lyrics.” The moment the writer starts proving they can do metaphors, the song puts on a blazer and stops fitting in the room.

Speech first, literature second

A good line often looks suspiciously ordinary. You can imagine it in a voice note. The rhyme doesn’t have to stick out at the end, the grammar can be a little crooked, and the thought can change its mind halfway through the sentence.

alive
“I’m already in bed, but somehow my phone’s in my hand again.”
written
“In the stillness of the night, the screen preserves the remnants of our closeness.”

An object is stronger than a feeling

“I’m sad” reports a state. “I put our song on again and turned it straight off” shows behavior. The listener will fill in the rest.

Weak

“I feel empty without you.”

Stronger

“You’ve still got my charger. I’m into week three of not texting to ask for it back.”

Weak

“I find it hard to forget the past.”

Stronger

“I deleted the chat, but I remember where your photo was in it.”

You can crumple the syntax

Short asides help: “okay,” “so what,” “honestly,” “actually, no.” They make a thought feel like it just appeared. But if every line is stuffed with them, the lyrics start imitating live speech instead of being it.

The genre’s best words are often the ones the writer first wanted to cross out for being too simple.

Four operations

01
Remove the diagnosis

“I’m jealous” → show the third visit to the same profile.

02
Remove the scenery

If neon and rain change nothing, they don’t have to be there.

03
Bring back the awkward word

“Stupid,” “that’s it,” and “anyway” are sometimes more alive than their literary equivalents.

04
Read it aloud

If your tongue stumbles where it shouldn’t, the line isn’t ready yet.

check

Turn the music off. If the phrase still sounds like something a person would say, the language has held up.
