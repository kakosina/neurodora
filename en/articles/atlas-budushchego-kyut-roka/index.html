An atlas of cute rock’s future

The genre will get more interesting when it stops sounding like one band. Eight branches you can start building right now.

mapThe borders are open.

This isn’t an official classification. More of a menu: which sides of the genre you could turn up further.

The useful thing about the term “cute rock” is that it already contains a conflict. That makes it easy to bolt on a third force and see what breaks.

Cute punk

Faster, shorter, dirtier. Fewer layers, more attack. The vocals stay melodic; the band plays like it has two minutes left on the rehearsal room rental.

risk

Sliding into ordinary pop punk and leaving the “cute” entirely to the singer’s look.

Cute shoegaze

A sweet voice almost drowns in a wall of guitars. The lyrics get a little less conversational, but a sense of dreams and distance creeps in.

Cute J-rock

More harmonic movement, an active bassline, bright guitar responses, a very singable chorus. Don’t copy an anime opening — borrow how fast it thinks.

Five more branches

04Cute grunge

Lower tuning, less shine, more exhaustion.

05Cute hyperpop

Wrecked vocals, digital distortion, abrupt cuts. Don’t lose the song inside the effects.

06Cute DnB

Fast electronic low end plus guitars in the chorus.

07Cute acoustic

Testing the material without the volume.

08Cute noise

A beautiful melody that noise is trying to eat.

??The next branch

The name is up for grabs. That’s the whole point.

A subgenre becomes real when people start arguing about whether it has the right to call itself a subgenre.

the rule of the map

Let the way you tell the story change along with the sound. Otherwise, it’s just a new guitar preset.
