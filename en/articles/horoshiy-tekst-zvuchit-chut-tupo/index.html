Good lyrics sound a little dumb

Simple doesn’t mean empty.

Sometimes a line only seems too ordinary because the writer is used to hiding behind pretty words.

Some lines are embarrassing to show anyone before you record them. On paper, they look almost ridiculous. Then the vocal hits the right note, the band comes in, and you realize making them more complicated would have ruined them.

Directness is a risk

“I want you to come over” looks simpler than “I miss your presence.” But the first phrase isn’t hiding. It’s easy to sing loud and easy to feel if it lands in your own situation.

Where simplicity becomes stupidity

When a phrase has no detail of its own. “I’m miserable without you” belongs to everyone. “I bought two Cokes out of habit” already has an author.

empty
“I love you and I can’t forget you.”
simple
“I changed my password, and it’s still your birthday.”

Smart simplicity is almost invisible

A simple line can contain a character, a past, an action in the present, and self-mockery. It just doesn’t list them. That makes lyrics like these harder than they look: the writer has to know more than they tell.

Simple words

Don’t mean a simple thought.

A short phrase

Doesn’t mean there’s no subtext.

A funny detail

Doesn’t cancel out a serious emotion.

Repetition

Isn’t lazy if the meaning changes.

A line has the right to look stupid. The problem is when it knows nothing beyond its own simplicity.

How to edit

Don’t automatically swap a simple word for a “beautiful” one. First find out what isn’t working: rhythm, imagery, precision, cliché. Sometimes one object is enough. Sometimes you need to cut the explanation.

the danger point

After the tenth edit, lyrics often become more correct and worse. Keep the early version.
