Cute rock will outlive every genre

Genres usually die of their own form

Every genre has a favorite trap. First, it finds a trick that works. Then a hundred people repeat it. Then a thousand. Then people start recognizing the genre faster than the song.

Cute rock works differently. It has no single mandatory drum pattern, type of guitar, or tempo. Its core isn’t in the arrangement, but in the collision of things we usually put in separate rooms: cute and noisy, everyday and enormous, funny and painful.

So you can ruin cute rock, but it’s hard to exhaust it. Bored with pop punk? It can crawl into shoegaze. Bored with shoegaze? Into electronic music. Bored with electronic music? Back to the garage. Everything on the outside changes. Inside, one principle remains: the feeling has to be closer than the pose.

Cute rock is immortal not because it can’t be killed. But because after every death, it can come back different.

Cute rock is a parasitic genre. And that’s a compliment

The best musical cultures know how to steal. Hip-hop stole samples, rock stole from the blues, and pop steals from absolutely everyone and acts like it was always that way.

Cute rock can do the same, only even more shamelessly. It takes speed from punk, hooks from pop, raw nerves from emo, noise from shoegaze, sharpness from J-rock, overload from hyperpop, and the intimacy of home from bedroom pop. After all that, you can still say: yes, this is cute rock.

Because recognition doesn’t come from an instrument list. It comes from the song’s personality.

The law: if new cute rock sounds like a meticulous reconstruction of the old stuff, it’s an archive, not the future.

A hundred years from now, it could still sound contemporary

A genre’s biggest problem is when you can’t update it without it feeling like a betrayal. Cute rock barely has that constraint.

It doesn’t have to preserve the sound of 2019. Quite the opposite: if cute rock sounds exactly the same in twenty years, the genre has failed.

The right kind of future cute rock should scare people who only like old cute rock. Then everything is going to plan.

Every genre becomes retro eventually. Cute rock could be the exception.
