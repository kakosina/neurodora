The forbidden vocabulary

Not a ban. A quarantine.

Any word on this list can be rescued by a concrete scene.

“Soul” won’t blow up the studio. “Forever” won’t call the police. But some words imitate depth so conveniently that the writer stops doing the rest of the work.

The red zone

souldestinyeternityabyssshards of a heartthe fire withinwingsforeverthrough the painagainst all odds

What’s wrong with them

They increase the scale too quickly. The writer hasn’t shown who argued with whom yet, and eternity has already arrived. The second problem is universality. “Shards of a heart” fit a million songs. A good detail usually fits one.

ready-made
“My soul shattered into pieces.”
your own
“I found your T-shirt and spent a minute deciding whether to wash it.”

How to get a pass

You can keep a red-zone word if the scene around it stops the phrase from becoming generic. The main question: if you replace it with another big word, does the song change?

The green zone

chargerapartment building entrancecasetaxikitchenstoriesticketsweatervoice notea broken hair clip

These aren’t mandatory props. It’s just that specific objects make the lyrics live in a time and place.

A big word asks for trust. A small detail earns it.

the house rule

If two red-zone words show up in four lines, rewrite one line around an action or an object.
