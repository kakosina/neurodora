Why cute rock needs a guitar

Switch it off.

If the song loses almost nothing when you switch the guitar off, the instrument may have been decoration.

The most useless way to use a guitar is to put it in the background just so the genre earns the right to call itself rock.

An instrument needs a job

The guitar is great at applying pressure and opening things up. In the verse, it’s dry, small, nervous. In the chorus, it takes up all the air. That contrast makes the emotion physical.

Four roles

A spring

A short, muted movement in the verse.

A wall

Big chords in the chorus.

A comment

A little melody after a vocal phrase.

A crash

Feedback, noise, a sudden stop, a breakdown.

Heavier doesn’t mean better

Too much distortion erases contrast. If the guitar fills the whole spectrum all the time, the chorus has no room to get bigger.

Sometimes an almost clean sound is more interesting. Sometimes cheap digital distortion is better than a collectible amp. Attack, air, and character matter more.

The guitar isn’t there to prove the genre. It should change the weather.

Three mistakes

Nothing but power chords. Useful as long as the rhythm is alive.

A sound that’s too correct. Perfect takes can turn a song into a plugin demo.

The guitar argues with the vocal. A riff needs to know when to step aside.

quick check

Leave the vocals, bass, and drums. Then bring the guitar back. If the contrast between verse and chorus gets noticeably stronger, the instrument is doing its job.
