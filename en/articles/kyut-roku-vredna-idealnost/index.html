Cute rock suffers from perfection

A slightly uneven take, noise, a breath, an unexpected pick attack can sometimes bring more life than another hour of alignment.

the dangerSterility.

When everything lines up perfectly with the grid, the person inside the song can disappear.

A modern studio can fix almost anything. Great—until we start fixing the things that made the recording interesting.

The human touch

A guitar coming in slightly early, a breath before the chorus, a voice turning rough on one word, one string struck harder than the others. These little things tell us: something happened.

What you can leave unfixed

Breathing

If it supports the rhythm of the phrase, don’t cut it automatically.

Uneven attack

A chorus can benefit from unevenness.

Noise

Feedback and the touch of strings are sometimes part of the gesture.

Tiny timing shifts

A live band doesn’t have to be a geometric exercise around the grid.

Deliberate dirt

Added vinyl crackle, artificial room noise, and an intentionally bad microphone don’t make a recording more honest. If you hear the trick before the song, it’s already a costume.

Dirt is good when there’s a reason for it. A “dirty” filter isn’t a reason.

What you absolutely should fix

An off note that distracts. A kick that accidentally breaks the groove. Noise that makes the lyrics inaudible. “Liveliness” isn’t a free pass for poor control.

before exporting

Save a version before the final cleanup and compare them the next day. The “raw” one sometimes already breathes better.
